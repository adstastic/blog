Quoting [Westenberg](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Op2m9AwHwmU&amp;si=HBhYwUWwl1KCio8o):

> The cult of goal setting converts uncertainty into an illusion of progress. It demands specificity in exchange for comfort. And it replaces selfrust with the performance of future planning.

> Constraints do not block creativity. They aim it.

> Setting goals feels like action. It gives you the warm sense of progress without the discomfort of change. You can spend hours calibrating, optimizing, refining your goals. You can build a notion dashboard. You can make a spreadsheet. You can go on a dopaminefueled productivity binge and still never do anything meaningful. Because goals are often surrogates for clarity. We set goals when we&#39;re uncertain about what we really want. The

goal becomes a placeholder. It acts as a proxy for direction, not a result of it.

> constraints make solutions not obvious, they force the kind of second order thinking that goals actively discourage. Instead of aiming for a finish line, the constrained mind seeks viability. It doesn&#39;t ask how do I get there? It asks what&#39;s possible from here. A goal set at time t is a bet on the future from position of ignorance. The more volatile the domain, the more brittle that bet becomes. This is where smart people get stuck. The brighter you are, the more coherent your plans tend to look on paper. But plans are scripts and reality is improvisation.

> Constraints scale

better because they don&#39;t assume knowledge. They are adaptive and they respond to feedback. A small team that decides we will not hire until we have product market fit has created a constraint that guides decisions without locking in predictions. A founder who says, &#34;I will only build products I can explain to a teenager in 60 seconds,&#34; is using a constraint as a filtering mechanism. This is the psychology of anti- goals.

> There&#39;s a kind of internal rebellion that occurs when you set a goal you don&#39;t believe in. You drag your heels, you sabotage yourself, and you procrastinate, but not because you&#39;re lazy, because you&#39;re not aligned. Anti-

goals are constraints disguised as aversions.

> The entrepreneur who says, &#34;I never want to work with clients who drain me,&#34; is sketching a boundary around their time, energy, and even identity. And it&#39;s not a goal. It&#39;s a refusal. Refusals shape lives just as powerfully as ambitions.

> John Boyd again. In his famous decision fork, he posed a career-defining question. Do you want to be someone or do you want to do something? Goals often come from the

first desire. Constraints come from the second. One is about image and the other is about identity.
