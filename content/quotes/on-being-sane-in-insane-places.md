Quoting [Quinn Barry](https://x.com/quinnmbarry/status/2052181863020810308/?s=12&amp;rw_tt_thread=True):

> But the solution when a set of facts conflicts with your existing beliefs is to think harder about them, not operate in realities in which those facts do not exist.

> My suspicion is that, if you walk through the logic tree, aligning a super-intelligence to human values is neither possible (“human values” is not a precise concept) nor desirable (a true super-intelligent AI should outperform humans on moral questions). But since people feel urgently compelled to “solve alignment,” there is a fear-driven drive to ignore premise-setting questions and start doing something, without looking at whether the underlying approach makes sense

> Moral anti-realism, then, means that humans have no responsibility for the future, since no one future is objectively better or worse than another. However, the entire premise of the X-risk community (filled with moral anti-realists) is that some futures *are* better than others, which is why reducing X-risk matters!

> In insane environments, normal behavior can seem insane.

> If you take uncomfortable facts seriously, trace reasoning to first principles, and resist pattern matching, you have a chance of maintaining your sanity in an insane world.
